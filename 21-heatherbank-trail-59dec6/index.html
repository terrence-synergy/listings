Section 08 · The second option

Staying in the home and accessing the equity.

A reverse mortgage lets homeowners aged 55 and over borrow against the home with no monthly payments. Interest is added to the balance and compounds, and the loan is repaid when the home is sold, when the owners move out permanently, or from the estate. Title stays with the owners and the lender guarantees the balance can never exceed the home's value. Because the advance is a loan rather than income, it is not taxed.

Two structures are shown below at 6.5%, with the home rising 2% a year. Lenders require the home to be the borrowers' principal residence and treat an extended absence as a repayment trigger, so eligibility needs to be confirmed with the lender before this option is relied on.

In short

Selling converts the full equity to cash now, at today's value, and ends the family's exposure to this house; the trade-off is the move, and giving up any future rise in value. A reverse mortgage keeps the family at Heatherbank and releases tax-free cash, at a real cost: about $555,000 of interest over ten years on a lump sum, or about $161,000 on the monthly draw, paid from the home's value when it is eventually sold. On the numbers alone, selling gives the family the most value with the least uncertainty; the case for staying rests on how much the home itself matters and how long the family expects to be in it.

Section 09 · Worth a closer look

A few things to work through before anything is signed.

These are flags, not advice. Each one is work our in-house professionals do as part of the file.

Principal residenceThe home qualifies for the whole period of ownership: it was never rented, the owners lived in it at various points, and their child ordinarily inhabited it while studying at university. Because the owners have been non-residents of Canada for tax purposes for part of that time, the portion of the gain the exemption shelters depends on their residency status in each year. We will go through the residency history year by year and work towards the fullest exemption available.
Clearance certificateA sale by non-resident owners requires a CRA clearance certificate (Form T2062). Until it is issued, the buyer's lawyer holds back 25% of the price in trust — about $343,750 at the midpoint. It should be filed as soon as an offer is accepted, and it is also where the principal residence claim is made.
Reverse mortgage eligibilityLenders require the home to be the borrowers' principal residence and expect the owners to live in it. An extended absence, generally more than six months in a year, can be treated as a permanent move and trigger repayment. The terms of any absence need to be settled with the lender before Option 2 is relied on.

Who buys a home like yours

Three buyers, and the one your home is built for.

We looked at every detached sale of 2,800 sq ft or more across Rouge E11, Rouge E10, Highland Creek and Centennial this year — twenty homes. The pattern in what sold tells us who to market to.

Two thirds of them had finished basement bedrooms, and a third had a second kitchen. That is a multi-generational market, and your finished lower level and 60-foot lot speak directly to it.

The multi-generational family

Thirteen of the twenty large homes sold this year had basement bedrooms; seven had a second kitchen. These buyers are housing parents or adult children and need the square footage, the parking and a lower level that works. This is your strongest buyer — and your finished basement is exactly what they search for.

The move-up family from within Scarborough

Selling a 1,500–2,000 sq ft home nearby and trading up without leaving the schools or the community. They are price-sensitive on the gap, not the absolute number, which is why a sharp list price pulls them in. The cul-de-sac and Rouge Valley setting matter to this buyer more than finishes do.

The renovator

Buying the largest footprint on the best lot they can find and finishing it to their own taste. They pay for size, lot and location, and discount for décor. Your 3,307 sq ft on a 60-foot cul-de-sac lot is precisely what they are hunting. 4 Atrium Lane, unfinished basement and all, sold to this buyer for $166,000 over asking.

Questions sellers ask

The things most people want to ask, answered before you have to.

What if it doesn't sell?

Then we adjust on evidence, not nerves. You get showing and traffic data weekly, and we agreed in advance what a slow first two weeks means. Most homes that stall were mispriced at launch or shown badly — both of which we handle before day one.

Why not just list high and see what happens?

Because the market reads it. 81 Dalmatian opened at $1,480,000 and took 90 days and $230,000 in reductions to sell. 4 Atrium opened sharply and sold for $166,000 over asking in ten days. The first ten days carry the most buyer attention you will ever get — the price has to be right when it matters.

Can I just do it myself, or use a discount brokerage?

You can. What you'd be taking on is the preparation, the photography, the ad spend, the showings, the negotiation and the paperwork — and carrying the risk on all of it. Our packages exist so you can choose how much of that you want handled.

How long does it actually take?

From the decision to list, about two weeks of preparation, then a firm sale typically in 30 to 60 days. Closing is usually 30 to 90 days after that, and the date is negotiable to suit you.

What will I actually walk away with?

Use the slider in the net proceeds section — it is your real numbers at any price. The only thing not shown is your mortgage payout, which your lender confirms in writing.

Do I pay anything if we don't sell?

No. Commission is earned on a completed sale. Staging, painting and photography at the Signature and Concierge levels are ours to carry.

Should I renovate first?
