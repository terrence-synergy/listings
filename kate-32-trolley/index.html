The tax plan: move your savings through an FHSA and RRSP first

Your $50,000 is going toward this purchase anyway. Routing $46,000 of it through registered accounts first turns it into tax deductions. You then withdraw it tax-free when you buy.

  1. Before December 31, 2026$8,000into a new FHSA

    Deducted on your 2026 return. Withdrawn tax-free for a first home, never repaid.

  2. January 2027$8,000into the FHSA again

    Your 2027 room. This one is deducted on your 2027 return, so it is not in the refund below.

  3. As soon as possible$30,000into your RRSP

    Deducted on your 2026 return. Withdrawn under the Home Buyers' Plan after 90 days in the account.

Where the 2026 refund comes from

Your $77,250 is base salary. With the overtime you have earned so far this year, 2026 income is projected at $89,238, assuming regular pays and no further overtime to year end. More overtime means a slightly larger refund.

Still to come on your 2027 return: about $2,370 from the January FHSA deposit and about $1,400 from the first-time home buyers' amount. Neither is counted in the numbers on this page.

Timeline

The RRSP deposit sets the pace, because that money has to sit for 90 days before it can come out for the purchase.

  1. Now to December 31, 2026Open an FHSA and deposit $8,000. Deposit $30,000 to your RRSP. Confirm your RRSP room on CRA My Account first.
  2. January 2027Deposit the second $8,000 to the FHSA.
  3. Late February 2027File your 2026 return once your T4 and contribution slips arrive. Refunds usually arrive about two weeks after e-filing.
  4. 90 days after the RRSP depositEarliest date to withdraw the $30,000 under the Home Buyers' Plan. A deposit on November 20 clears on February 18.
  5. Closing
  6. 2032 onwardRepay the RRSP $2,000 a year for 15 years. Nothing is repaid on the FHSA.

What these numbers assume

  • Mortgage: a 5-year variable rate of 3.95%, held at that rate for the whole projection. A variable rate moves with your lender's prime rate, so the payment or the interest share of it can change. With less than 20% down the mortgage is insured; the premium is added to the mortgage and Ontario's 8% sales tax on it is paid at closing.
  • Qualifying uses base salary of $77,250, a qualifying rate of 5.95% (your rate plus 2%), a 39% housing-cost limit, and no other debt. Overtime may count with a two-year history.
  • Tax: 2026 federal and Ontario rates, including the Ontario Health Premium and low-income workers credit. It assumes at least $30,000 of unused RRSP room and that you have not owned a home in the last four years.
  • Estimates: $30 a month for condo insurance, $25 for tenant insurance, $2,200 legal fees and title insurance, $400 closing adjustments.
  • Growth: rent rises 1.9% a year (Ontario's 2027 guideline), maintenance fees and property tax 3% a year. Selling costs are 4% plus $1,500 legal.
  • Unit 502 is tenant-occupied. Moving in depends on the seller delivering the unit vacant, which needs to be written into the offer.
  • These are estimates for planning. They are not a mortgage approval or a filed tax return.
